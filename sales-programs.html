Overview

One program: content, milestones, and a revenue outcome

A Sales Program is a record in Salesforce that bundles three things together: the exercises a rep completes (videos, lessons, practice pitches), the milestones that measure whether their behavior in the CRM has changed, and the outcome — the revenue metric the whole program is designed to move.

The gap

Traditional enablement can't prove it works

Most sales teams run enablement through spreadsheets, slide decks, and LMS links that live outside Salesforce. The problems are predictable.

Disconnected from the job. Content arrives before it's relevant, so reps consume it, forget it, and need it again later.
Hard to track. You can measure completions and views, but you can't tie those numbers to deal size or cycle time.
No feedback loop. If a program isn't working, you don't know which part failed or when.
Start with the outcome

Design backward from the revenue metric you want to move

Sales Programs asks you to name a specific, measurable outcome before you touch content. The four most common metrics are ramp time, win rate, cycle length, and deal size.

Ramp time. A new hire closes their first deal in 60 days — count Closed Won opportunities in a time window.
Win rate. Increase from 10% to 15% — divide deals won by total opportunities in a period.
Cycle length. Close in under 60 days — count opportunities that close within the target window.
Deal size. Average above $50K — count opportunities closing above the threshold.
Milestones

Milestones measure behavior, not content completion

A milestone is a goal inside the program that tracks real CRM activity — logged calls, booked meetings, sent emails — rather than whether someone clicked through a slide deck. Each milestone is backed by an Enablement Measure: a saved query against any standard or custom Salesforce object.

Task (Subtype = Call)Count calls logged against a target — e.g., "30 calls by day 10." Event (Type = Sales Call)Count meetings booked — e.g., "1 meeting by day 10." Opportunity (StageName)Count opportunities that reach a stage — e.g., "5 deals in Proposal." Custom objectsAny object in the org can back a measure — log cadences, survey responses, anything.
Prebuilt measures ship with the license; copy them rather than editing them, because changes affect every program using that measure.
Exercises

Exercises deliver content exactly when it's relevant

An exercise is the content piece a rep completes: a video, a lesson, a document, or a practice pitch. Program Builder lets you drag and drop exercises onto a canvas alongside milestones, grouped into sections, each with a due date. Content appears in the Guidance Center — a side panel accessible from every page in Salesforce — so the rep never leaves their workflow.

VideoYouTube, Vidyard, or Vimeo link — auto-completes when watched. LessonRich text with estimated reading time — rep must click "Mark as complete." Document / AudioLink to a PDF or audio file — auto-completes on access. Feedback RequestRep submits a practice pitch; peers or Einstein Coach provide structured feedback. Action ItemAn offline task — shadow a call, attend a briefing — no link required. TrailheadLink to any public Trailhead module.
Program Builder

Building a program takes minutes, not weeks

Program Builder is a drag-and-drop canvas inside Lightning Experience. Eight ready-made templates cover the most common sales roles and outcomes, so you rarely start from scratch.

Templates included. BDR onboarding, AE onboarding, SE onboarding, CSM onboarding, Build a Strong Pipeline, Increase Deal Size, Win More Opportunities, Sell a New Product.
Publish and assign. Assign users manually, share for self-enrollment, or automate via Flow, Apex, or REST API — including triggers based on KPI misses.
Notifications built in. In-app and email reminders for program assignment, overdue milestones, approaching due dates, and completion.
Org limits: 300 programs (published or unpublished) and 100 measures. Templates are only available for internal Sales Programs, not Partner Tracks.
Analytics

Every program generates a dashboard that closes the loop

Publishing a program automatically creates a prebuilt dashboard and a set of reports. The dashboard shows, at a glance, how many reps are on track for each milestone and outcome — and where they're falling behind.

Leading indicators. Milestone completion during the program shows whether coaching is landing before the outcome window closes.
Lagging indicators. Outcome completion at the end reveals whether behavior change actually moved the revenue metric.
Iterate. If reps complete exercises and milestones but the revenue metric doesn't shift, the model tells you which assumption to question next.
Clone the prebuilt dashboard before customizing. Editing it in place removes your ability to reset to the Salesforce default.

    What it means for you

    For AEs and reps

    Guidance surfaces when you need it

    The Guidance Center appears in a side panel on every Salesforce page. You don't switch tabs or search for content — it shows up when it's due, tied to what you're doing in your pipeline right now.

    For enablement leaders

    Prove ROI with data you already have

    Measures point at objects already in your CRM — Tasks, Events, Opportunities — so there's no separate data pipeline to build. Program analytics arrive the moment you publish.

    For sales managers

    Automate coaching when a KPI slips

    A Flow can watch for a rep missing a milestone — say, fewer than five calls in week one — and automatically assign a remediation program. Coaching happens without a manager having to catch it manually.

    Three things people get wrong

    Myth

    "It's just an LMS inside Salesforce"

    A traditional LMS tracks content consumption. Sales Programs tracks CRM behavior and ties it to the revenue metric you started from. Content is present, but it's a means to the outcome, not the measure of success.

    Myth

    "We need to rebuild all our content"

    Existing videos, PDFs, and slide decks can be linked directly into exercises. The Enablement workspace holds links and rich text; it doesn't replace your content repository.

    Myth

    "Measures require a developer to set up"

    Prebuilt measures ship with the license and cover the most common activities. Custom measures are created through a point-and-click wizard — pick an object, add field filters, and preview with live data before saving.
